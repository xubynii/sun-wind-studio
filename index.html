Massing efficiency

Energy efficiency read from the form alone, before any envelope or system is chosen: form factor and surface-to-volume ratio, a heat-loss index at typical construction, compactness against a square prism of the same height and volume, façade orientation balance, daylit floor area, and exposed roofs and soffits. The review on the right updates live as you shape the massing.

How to read it

Ratings A to E are bands for supertall towers. A compact shaft rates A on form factor at 0.30 m² of envelope per m² of floor; articulated towers with voids and terraces drift toward D and E, and the heat-loss ratio says what that costs against the equivalent square prism. Daylit area counts floor within 6 m of a façade or void. Design development carries these into the full energy model with the façade zones, construction and systems.

Energy

A monthly energy balance per program zone in the manner of ISO 13790: envelope losses by orientation from the carved floors, ventilation with heat recovery, internal gains on program schedules, and solar gains through the window-to-wall ratio, SHGC and shading devices of each façade zone. Results are read against Vancouver's rezoning targets for TEUI, TEDI and GHGI and update live as you edit.

Systems and loads

Programs are derived from the massing by plate size and height (office, housing, hotel, retail, public, plant). Window-to-wall ratio, SHGC and device transmission come from the Façade tab; the device transmission uses the last sun run where there is one.

Façade

Design the façade by zone: a face over a range of levels, with window size, glass, an external shading device, PV and operable openings. The model and the floor plan in the review redraw as you edit; the solar heat table re-reads the last sun run instantly.

Daylight

Read against LEED v4.1 EQ Daylight: spatial daylight autonomy sDA300/50% at 40, 55 and 75% of floor for 1, 2 and 3 points, with annual sunlight exposure ASE1000/250h held at or under 10%. Here sDA is a daylight-factor proxy and ASE a sun sweep; the solar heat and glare of each zone come from the same run.
